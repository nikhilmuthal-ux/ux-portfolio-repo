Dart Charge

Pay before it becomes a fine.

Every Dartford Crossing costs £3.50, due by midnight the next day. Miss it and it becomes £70. I researched why people were fined, and designed the iOS app that warns them in time.

Context

A toll with no tollbooth, and a deadline written as a rule.

Cameras read every plate, and the only warning is a road sign passed at speed. Most people first meet the service through a penalty notice.

The Dartford Crossing. Barriers came down in 2014, cameras read plates at speed.

160,000

Vehicles a day, at a crossing built for 135,000.

£2.50 → £70

One day late, at the time of the research. Up to £105 if ignored.

277,000

Penalty notices a month, 2024 peak.

Penalty figures: 2024 public reporting (Fleet News, National Highways), not part of the research.

Reframe

The brief said payment. The real job was the obligation.

The service already knew the crossing, the plate, the date and the balance. It just never said so in time. Faster forms would not have closed a single gap.

Tell people what they owe and by when, before it becomes a fine.

Final design

The shipped app, refined.

My final design next to what Conduent's team shipped (v1.3). Six changes on the start screen alone, and every screen works, so tap through it.

What changed

  1. Native iOS feelSystem font, a calm photo and a chevron on every row.
  2. The deadline up front“Pay by midnight the day after you cross” replaces a generic hint.
  3. Correct wording“Set up an account”, not “Setup”.
  4. Plain hintsNo more “Click here to…” in small italics.
  5. Says what you get“Crossing reminders”, not “Receive Notifications”.
  6. Sentence case“Sign in”, not spaced capitals.

02 · No surprises at payment

The price, the total and the late payment rule, before you pay.

Problem

The whole penalty system rested on one rule, written as a sentence. In the app it arrived as a pop-up that blocked payment, and the total was easy to miss.

So the design

The rule, the price and the total sit on the step itself, so nothing surprises you at payment.

On screen
  1. The late payment rule, inline, not a pop-up.
  2. Where you are, step two of four.
  3. Plus and minus, no typing.
  4. Price each and the total update together.
  5. Actions pinned in thumb reach.

Trade offs

What I turned down, each one cheaper to build.

01

Turned downOne unified wizard for everyone

ChoseSplit entry

Quick pay is the main action and accounts are a separate path. The wizard repeated the exact step where people gave up.

02

Turned downA deadline banner for everyone

ChoseA calm hint where you pay

Most people opening the app have not just crossed. The rule sits on the payment option, and each claimed crossing and penalty shows its own date.

Turned downFull account lookup by plate

ChoseCrossing status only

Showing nothing personal is what lets the check work without a login.

Nobody reconciles forty vehicles on a phone. Half serving fleets would have made the app worse for everyone. Testing before handover confirmed it.

What the reviews tell us

★★★★★When I call customer service, they advise me to download the app.

The call centre sends people to the app. We surveyed the call centre twice during research. Today its own staff point callers to the app.

★★★★★Finally a quicker way for us to top up or pay. Clearly laid out and easy to use.

Paying is the job. What drivers praise is the thing the research put first: a fast, clear way to pay.

★★★★☆Needs a different password from the internet account. If your biometric login expires, you can't log back in without deleting the app.

Sign in still fails people. The final design adds a reset by emailed code, so nobody has to delete the app to get back in. It is the next flow I would test.

Looking back

I started by polishing the payment form. The research taught me to find where people fail before I design a single screen.

Do differently

Find the failure before fixing the screen.

The gap analysis should have come before any interface work, not after it.

Do differently

Finish what you cut.

The hire car route was cut early. It came back in the final design as Claim a crossing, and should not have left.

Hardest call

Leaving fleets out.

Still right. Their work is desktop work, and the web service already serves it.

Keep

The coverage audit.

Tying every screen to a numbered problem cut three features that could not name one.

Carried forward

Trace every screen to a numbered problem. I now use the same problem to screen mapping in my TIBCO Control Plane work, so every design decision can say what it fixes.

Heuristic review

Two showstoppers: an unexplained login email, and no contact number anywhereSoloLed toD01D02D05
+

Grading every finding let us order the roadmap on evidence, not taste.

2Showstoppers

Seriously impair use, and cannot be overcome by the user.

3Major problems

Make people quit the task they are doing.

4Irritants

Slow people down or break common standards.

ShowstopperH4, H5, H10

The account setup confirmation captures an email, but nothing says it is used to log in. No validation for mistyped details, and no email verification.

A forgiving email pattern, and verification as part of sign up.

ShowstopperH3, H5

Contact appears on only two pages, never highlighted, and there is no contact centre number anywhere on the site.

A helpline, and help people can use to solve the problem themselves.

Major problemH5, H9, H10

No required fields, and no error message names the error or suggests a fix. People cannot tell what went wrong.

Mark required fields, and explain every error with a way to fix it.

Major problemH3, H5, H10

The number of steps in payment and account setup is never shown, and one colour is used for every state.

Label every step and show status with colour.

Major problemH4, H5

Two names for one task: create an account and set up an account. Highlights read as payment notices.

One vocabulary, and colour reserved for meaning.

IrritantH3, H5, H10

On the no charge screen people must log in before paying, and account creation has no way out once started.

Let people pay without an account, and give every flow an exit.

IrritantH1, H4, H8, H10

Balance and account number look like every other field, and data is cluttered rather than grouped.

Bold the balance and account number, group the rest.

IrritantH4, H5, H10

Back and next buttons change position and look between tasks.

Fix button positions once, everywhere.

IrritantH3, H5, H7, H10

The current stage is unreadable except on account setup.

Make the stage explicit on every screen.
